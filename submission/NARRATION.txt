This is CallReclaim. The demo uses fictional records, and the workflow stops at owner review.

When a detailing shop misses a call between jobs, the useful details can get buried across a missed-call alert, a consent record, and a text conversation. This desk gives an agent four WebMCP tools for preparing that work without making the owner's decision.

I ask the agent to compare the consented leads. It gets a short, structured list with each request's age, urgency, and sample job value, then chooses the paint-correction inquiry.

Inspecting that lead opens the same conversation I see. The agent receives the caller's message and the facts already recorded on the page: a black SUV, swirl marks, an estimate request, and a deadline before Saturday.

Next, it drafts a reply from those details. The text appears in my editor as revision one. I can change it, discard it, or leave it alone.

When the agent queues that revision, the draft moves to my review. If I edit it, the queue reopens and the old revision is rejected. That prevents the agent from acting on work I have already changed.

The final record has no follow-up consent. A drafting attempt fails, and the page stays unchanged.

That is the point of CallReclaim: let the agent sort the conversation and prepare a response, then leave the judgment with the person who owns the customer relationship.
